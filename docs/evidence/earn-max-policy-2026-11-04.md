## Optional debt reduction

The five October 29 ONyc sales returned $235,414.46 against $236,562.96 of adjacent oracle marks: $1,148.50 shortfall. At an unchanged 3.02-point adverse spread, retaining that much debt costs about $19.46/day. The sale expense equals 59.0 days of that carry.

A one-day hold therefore avoids $1,148.50 of **immediate** sale expense while adding approximately $19.46 carry cost in this scenario. It retains market and liquidation exposure and can defer sale costs into a later withdrawal. This is not a claim of permanent net savings.

## Slower withdrawals, smaller sales and diversification

November 4 sold $145,491.38 of collateral for $642.55 below its marks. **If** paced sales actually achieved 10–20 bps, the immediate expense would be $145.49–$290.98, a conditional saving of $351.57–$497.06 before waiting costs. No historical quotes prove that fill was available.

In a fee-free toy pool, selling $5,000 once costs $24.88; ten pieces without replenishment cost $24.88; ten pieces with complete replenishment between them cost $2.50. Real extra fees and waiting exposure can erase the improvement.

Diversification scenario: a 50% allocation cap bounds a loss confined to one lane at half the vault exposure; a correlated shock still hits all lanes. It does not prove lower aggregate execution cost. Compare executable exits across independent collateral, borrowing reserves and swap liquidity; two loops on the same collateral are not independent protection.

The local implementation selects a 10% reserve with a $5,000 floor and $25,000 cap, a seven-day economic horizon, and true economic 1x for Earn MAX. Cash is reserved from incoming funds; raising the target does not refill an empty buffer. These are candidate settings, not demonstrated optima. Nothing has been deployed. The existing withdrawal waiting period is 600 seconds; a longer wait, paced execution, withdrawal charges and concentration limits remain separate proposals in `docs/earn-max-loss-controls.md`.
